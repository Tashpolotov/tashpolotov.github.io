# Foam Tycoon — one-page pitch

**Genre:** Idle / management tycoon · **Engine:** Unity 6 (WebGL) · **Platform:** browser, instant-play (no download, no install, no login)
**Play now:** https://tashpolotov.github.io/foamtycoon/

## What it is
Foam Tycoon is a top-down idle tycoon about building a car-washing empire. You start as one person with a hose in a scruffy yard and grow, stage by stage, into the owner of a luxury car showroom. It is a hands-on idle game: you walk your character around, do the work yourself at first, then hire a team and let the money keep flowing while you expand.

## The idea
Most idle tycoons are pure numbers behind a menu. Foam Tycoon makes the core fantasy physical and visible: the dirt on each car is real and washes off in patches under your hose (a mask-shader effect), so you *see* progress on every single car instead of just watching a counter tick. That tactile "it's actually getting clean" feel is the hook.

## What the player does
- **Move and wash.** Drag or WASD to move; stand by a dirty car and scrub the grime off in real patches.
- **Earn and reinvest.** Collect cash, then upgrade hose power, team speed, income per car, and unlock new wash bays.
- **Hire and automate.** Washers, cashiers, soap/polish runners and mechanics take over the grind so the business runs itself.
- **Grow through 4 stages:** Yard hand-wash → Automatic tunnel wash → Detailing center → Car showroom (buy, clean and resell cars).
- **React to events:** rush hours (double cash), golden VIP cars worth 5x if you wash them yourself, and timed challenges.

## What makes it different
1. **Dirt that really washes off in patches** (mask shader) — not a progress bar.
2. **A 4-stage journey** with a genuine sense of rising from nothing to a car empire, each stage a new mini-game.
3. **Funny random clients** with emoji moods, tips, and impatient customers who drive off.
4. **Active, not passive idle:** helpers are deliberately slower than you, consumables (soap buckets, polish) must be restocked, and events reward hands-on play — so there is always something to do.

## Why it is fun
Short, satisfying feedback on every car; a clear "number goes up" reinvestment loop; and a visible status climb from a bucket in a yard to a showroom of luxury cars. Each of the 4 stages introduces a new system, so the ~80 minutes of core content (≈20 min per stage) stays fresh.

## Commercial potential (honest, pessimistic base case)
Idle/management is a proven, durable genre on web portals and Steam, but it is **saturated** and discovery is the hard part — this prototype was declined once by a major portal precisely for being a crowded genre, which is the realistic bar. A typical new portal idle game earns roughly **$50–1,500/month**, at about **$1–15 per 1,000 plays**, and only with meaningful traffic. Realistic plan: treat it as one title in a portfolio, lead with the "real dirt washing off" hook in the trailer and thumbnail to beat the discovery problem, and expect **$0 while unproven**. Revenue would scale only with retention and traffic, neither of which is guaranteed. No optimistic promises here — the upside depends entirely on a hook that stands out in a flooded category.

## 6-month plan to a full game
- **M1 — Depth:** rebalance the full 4-stage economy; prestige/rebirth loop; persistent meta-upgrades across stages.
- **M2 — Content:** 15–20 car types, seasonal/weather dirt, named recurring clients, mini-story beats between stages.
- **M3 — Feel & polish:** better VFX/SFX on washing, screen juice, tutorial, settings, 8–10 languages.
- **M4 — Monetization:** rewarded video (speed-up / x2 cash / offline x2) and one non-intrusive interstitial per stage; optional cosmetic IAP.
- **M5 — Live/soft-launch:** ship to 2–3 web portals + a Steam demo page; instrument retention (D1/D7), tune the first 10 minutes.
- **M6 — Scale:** data-driven balance, a 5th "franchise/map" stage, marketing creatives built around the washing hook.

## Made solo, with AI
Built by a single indie developer. Idea, game design, art direction and production are the developer's; the code was generated with AI (Claude Code / Claude) under the developer's direction. This is intentional: AI handles the implementation so one person can ship a complete, polished prototype.
