01What it is

Foam Tycoon is a top-down idle tycoon about building a car-washing empire.

You start as one person with a hose in a scruffy yard and grow, stage by stage, into the owner of a luxury car showroom. It is a hands-on idle game: you walk your character around and do the work yourself at first, then hire a team and let the money keep flowing while you expand. The idea, design, art direction and production are mine; the code was generated with AI.

02What the player does

  • Move and wash. Drag or WASD to move; stand by a dirty car and scrub the grime off in real patches.
  • Earn and reinvest. Collect cash, then upgrade hose power, team speed, income per car, and unlock new wash bays.
  • Hire and automate. Washers, cashiers, soap/polish runners and mechanics take over the grind so the business runs itself.
  • Grow through 4 stages. Yard hand-wash → automatic tunnel wash → detailing center → car showroom (buy, clean and resell cars).
  • React to events. Rush hours (double cash), golden VIP cars worth 5× if you wash them yourself, and timed challenges.

03What makes it different

  • Dirt that really washes off in patches (a mask shader) — not a progress bar. You see progress on every single car.
  • A 4-stage journey with a genuine rise from nothing to a car empire; each stage is a new mini-game.
  • Funny random clients with emoji moods, tips, and impatient customers who drive off.
  • Active, not passive idle. Helpers are deliberately slower than you, consumables (soap, polish) must be restocked, and events reward hands-on play — there is always something to do.

04Why it's fun

Short, satisfying feedback on every car; a clear "number goes up" reinvestment loop; and a visible status climb from a bucket in a yard to a showroom of luxury cars. Each of the four stages introduces a new system, so the ~80 minutes of core content (about 20 minutes per stage) stays fresh.

05Commercial potential

Honest, pessimistic base case. Idle/management is a proven, durable genre on web portals and Steam — but it is saturated, and discovery is the hard part. This prototype was already declined once by a major portal precisely for being a crowded genre, which is the realistic bar.

A typical new portal idle game earns roughly $50–1,500/month, at about $1–15 per 1,000 plays, and only with meaningful traffic. The realistic plan: treat it as one title in a portfolio, lead with the "real dirt washing off" hook in the trailer and thumbnail to beat the discovery problem, and expect $0 while unproven. Revenue scales only with retention and traffic, neither of which is guaranteed. No optimistic promises here — the upside depends entirely on a hook that stands out in a flooded category.

066-month plan to a full game

  • Month 1Depth. Rebalance the full 4-stage economy; add a prestige/rebirth loop and persistent meta-upgrades across stages.
  • Month 2Content. 15–20 car types, seasonal/weather dirt, named recurring clients, mini-story beats between stages.
  • Month 3Feel & polish. Better wash VFX/SFX, screen juice, tutorial, settings, 8–10 languages.
  • Month 4Monetization. Rewarded video (speed-up / x2 cash / offline x2) and one non-intrusive interstitial per stage; optional cosmetic IAP.
  • Month 5Soft-launch. Ship to 2–3 web portals plus a Steam demo page; instrument D1/D7 retention and tune the first 10 minutes.
  • Month 6Scale. Data-driven balance, a 5th "franchise/map" stage, marketing creatives built around the washing hook.
